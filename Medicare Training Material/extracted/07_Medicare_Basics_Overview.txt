ELIGIBILITY
Who Qualifies for Medicare?
You must generally be a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident who has lived in the U.S. for at least 5 years.
Primary Pathways
01
Age 65 or Older
Most common path. Automatic enrollment if already receiving Social Security benefits.
02
Disability
Under 65 and entitled to Social Security disability benefits for 24 months (waiting period may be waived).
03
ESRD
Any age with permanent kidney failure requiring dialysis or a kidney transplant.
04
ALS (Lou Gehrig's)
Any age diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis — no waiting period.
Premium-Free Part A
Most people get Part A at no monthly cost if they (or a spouse) paid Medicare taxes for at least 10 years (40 quarters) while working.
Key Notes
• Part B is optional but most people enroll
• You can buy Part A if you do not qualify for free coverage
• Enrollment is not always automatic — check your situation
• Spousal work history can help you qualify

PART A
Hospital Insurance — What It Covers & Costs (2026)
Most people receive premium-free Part A after paying Medicare taxes for at least 10 years.
What Part A Helps Cover
Inpatient Hospital Care
Semi-private room, meals, general nursing, drugs as part of inpatient treatment, and other hospital services.
Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF)
Care after a qualifying 3-day hospital stay. Days 1–20 fully covered after deductible; coinsurance applies days 21–100.
Hospice Care
For terminally ill patients. Includes pain relief, support services, and counseling. Minimal cost sharing.
Home Health Care
Part-time skilled nursing, physical/occupational therapy, and home health aide services when medically necessary.

PART B
Medical Insurance — What It Covers & Costs (2026)
Part B is optional but strongly recommended. Most people pay a monthly premium.
What Part B Helps Cover
•  Services from doctors and other health care providers
•  Outpatient hospital care and emergency room visits
•  Durable medical equipment (wheelchairs, walkers, oxygen)
•  Many preventive services (screenings, vaccines, wellness visits)
•  Clinical laboratory tests and diagnostic imaging
•  Mental health services (outpatient)
•  Ambulance services and some home health care
•  Limited prescription drugs administered by a provider

COVERAGE CHOICES
Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage (Part C)
You choose one path when you first enroll and can switch during certain periods each year.
Original Medicare
Parts Included
Part A + Part B (add Part D separately)
Provider Choice
Any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare nationwide
Referrals
Generally not required for specialists
Extra Benefits
Limited — vision, dental, hearing usually not covered
Out-of-Pocket
No annual maximum; Medigap can help fill gaps
Drug Coverage
Optional separate Part D plan
Medicare Advantage (Part C)
Parts Included
Bundled A + B, usually includes Part D
Provider Choice
Must use plan network (HMO/PPO rules apply)
Referrals
Often required for specialists (especially HMOs)
Extra Benefits
Many plans offer dental, vision, hearing, gym, OTC
Out-of-Pocket
Annual maximum (varies by plan); more predictable costs
Drug Coverage
Usually included; check formulary carefully

PART D
Prescription Drug Coverage
Optional coverage for outpatient prescription drugs. Available as a standalone plan or through most Medicare Advantage plans.
How It Works
Private insurance companies offer plans that follow Medicare rules. You pay a monthly premium and share costs for drugs.
Formulary
Each plan has a list of covered drugs (formulary). Check that your medications are covered before enrolling.
2026 OOP Cap
Thanks to the Inflation Reduction Act, out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026.
Extra Help (LIS)
People with limited income and resources may qualify for the Low-Income Subsidy, which lowers premiums and costs.

FILLING THE GAPS
Medigap, Medicaid & Extra Help
Original Medicare leaves cost-sharing gaps. Several programs can reduce what you pay.
Medigap (Medicare Supplement)
Private policies that help pay deductibles, coinsurance, and copayments under Original Medicare. Standardized plans (A–N). Best bought during the 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period starting when Part B begins.
Medicaid / Dual Eligibility
State-federal program for people with limited income and resources. Dual eligibles can get help with Medicare premiums and cost-sharing, plus extra benefits such as long-term care and transportation.
Medicare Savings Programs
State programs that help pay Part A and/or Part B premiums and sometimes deductibles and coinsurance for people with modest income and resources.
Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy)
Federal program that helps pay Part D premiums, deductibles, and copayments. Expanded eligibility under recent legislation. Apply through Social Security.

GETTING STARTED
Enrollment Periods & Key Decisions
Timing matters. Missing your initial enrollment window can result in lifelong late-enrollment penalties.
Initial Enrollment Period
•  7-month window around 65th birthday
•  3 months before + month of + 3 after
•  Best time to avoid penalties
•  Sign up for A and/or B
Annual Election Period
•  Oct 15 – Dec 7 every year
•  Switch between Original & Advantage
•  Change or join Part D plans
•  Changes take effect Jan 1
Special Enrollment
•  Still working past 65 with employer coverage
•  Moving, losing other coverage
•  Qualifying life events
•  Avoids late-enrollment penalties

## Slide 11
MEDICARE BASICS  ·  DETAILED OVERVIEW  ·  2026
SUMMARY
Key Takeaways
Remember these essentials when navigating Medicare.
1
Four Parts
A (Hospital), B (Medical), C (Advantage), D (Drugs). Understand what each covers before choosing.
2
Two Main Paths
Original Medicare + optional Medigap/Part D, or a Medicare Advantage plan that bundles coverage.
3
Costs Are Shared
Premiums, deductibles, and coinsurance apply. 2026 standard Part B premium is $202.90; Part A is free for most.
4
Help Is Available
Extra Help, Medicare Savings Programs, Medicaid, and Medigap can significantly lower your out-of-pocket costs.
5
Timing Matters
Enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period to avoid permanent late penalties. Review coverage every year.
6
Official Sources
Always verify details at Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE. Plans and costs change annually.
