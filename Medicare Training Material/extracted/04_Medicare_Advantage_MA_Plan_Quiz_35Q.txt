## Slide 39
Answer Key — Questions 1–7
Q1: C
Medicare Advantage is also called Medicare Part C.
Q2: C
Eligibility requires both Medicare Part A and Part B.
Q3: B
Private insurers contract with Medicare to offer MA plans.
Q4: B
Most MA plans are MA-PDs and include Part D drug coverage.
Q5: B
MA plans are required to cap annual out-of-pocket spending for Part A and B services.
Q6: C
CMS set the 2025 in-network MOOP maximum at $9,350.
Q7: B
HMOs generally require in-network care and referrals for specialists.
SPEAKER NOTES: 39

## Slide 40
Answer Key — Questions 8–14
Q8: C
You cannot have Medigap while enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan.
Q9: B
AEP runs October 15 through December 7 each year.
Q10: B
MA OEP (Jan 1–Mar 31) allows one change: switch MA plans or return to Original Medicare.
Q11: C
Over half (around 54–55%) of eligible beneficiaries are enrolled in MA plans.
Q12: A
Dental, vision, and hearing benefits are very common supplemental benefits in MA plans.
Q13: B
Medicare pays MA plans a fixed monthly amount (capitation) per enrollee.
Q14: B
SNPs serve dual-eligible individuals, those with chronic conditions, or institutionalized beneficiaries.

## Slide 41
Answer Key — Questions 15–21
Q15: B
HMOs generally restrict coverage to their network (with exceptions for emergencies).
Q16: B
MA plans must cover emergency and urgently needed services even out of network/area.
Q17: B
Rebates result when a plan’s bid is below the benchmark; they fund extra benefits or lower costs.
Q18: B
The previous ESRD enrollment restriction was removed; people with ESRD can generally join MA plans.
Q19: B
Zero-premium means $0 additional MA plan premium; the standard Part B premium still applies.
Q20: B
Prior authorization is commonly used by MA plans for certain procedures, DME, and therapies.
Q21: B
Hospice care is covered under Original Medicare even if the person remains in an MA plan.

## Slide 42
Answer Key — Questions 22–28
Q22: B
Many MA plans use set copays (e.g., $20 primary care) rather than 20% coinsurance.
Q23: B
The Initial Coverage Election Period allows first-time enrollment in an MA plan.
Q24: A
Original Medicare allows any provider that accepts Medicare, anywhere in the U.S.
Q25: B
Loss of plan or moving out of area triggers a Special Enrollment Period.
Q26: B
CMS contracts with and regulates Medicare Advantage organizations.
Q27: B
PPOs typically cover out-of-network services at higher cost-sharing levels.
Q28: B
D-SNPs are designed for dual-eligible beneficiaries (Medicare + Medicaid).

## Slide 43
Answer Key — Questions 29–35
Q29: C
A large majority of MA-PD enrollees are in plans with $0 additional premium.
Q30: C
Plans must provide at least the same overall value; individual services have cost-sharing limits set by CMS.
Q31: B
MOOP protects beneficiaries by limiting their annual out-of-pocket costs for covered services.
Q32: B
The MA OEP (January–March) allows one change for those already in MA.
Q33: B
CMS Star Ratings measure quality; higher-rated plans may receive bonus payments and attract more members.
Q34: A
MA plans must cover all medically necessary Part A and Part B services (with limited exceptions such as hospice).
Q35: B
The best choice depends on doctors/hospitals used, expected care needs, travel, and total cost exposure.
